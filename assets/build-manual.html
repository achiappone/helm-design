C. Assembly, in order

This sequence is verified by cad/assembly_check.py, which sweeps a real driver down every fastener and slides every part along the path it travels, judging each step against only the parts already fitted. Steps that must come first are marked; they are the ones that fail if you swap them.

  1. Tap the three fittings, cover on the bench

    • The gland, the vent and the coax entry all thread into printed blocks beside the bay bumps. Do this first, with the cover bare. Once the boards, the heatsink or the shroud are on, nothing reaches them.
    • Tap the −x bottom block M16×1.5 at x=-110 for the cable gland (drill is already 14.5).
    • Tap the +x bottom block M12×1.5 at x=133 for the Gore vent.
    • Tap the +x TOP block M8×0.75 at x=120 for the SMA. Drop the bulkhead’s nut into the 12 mm hex pocket on the inner face first — it is captive there and you will not be able to hold it later.
    • Run a smear of thread sealant on each and nip them up. All three point downward or upward, never aft, so none of them can stand in water.

  2. Bond the heatsink into its seat

    • The heatsink goes in from the inside: its base drops into the 74 × 150 pocket in the cover’s inner face and its fins pass through the aperture to stand 4 mm proud outside.
    • Trim the fins back 8 mm all round first. A bought extrusion carries fins to the edge of its base; those outer fins are what the seat and the bond land on.
    • Degrease both faces with IPA. Use a flexible adhesive — MS polymer or Sikaflex 291i — not epoxy: aluminium and ASA move 0.9 mm differently over 150 mm and 50 °C, and a rigid bond will let go.
    • The pocket walls index it, so there is no jig. Clamp light and leave it.

  3. Boards into their bays

    • Both boards drop in from the inside of the cover and sit on printed bosses.
    • −x bay: Raspberry Pi 4 on four M2.5 × 6 into the 5 mm bosses, connector stack facing the bay’s long axis.
    • +x bay: the 68 × 27 LCD driver board on its four M2.5, then the RTL-SDR standing on edge in the 19 mm strip beside it, strapped to the two tie anchors on the bay floor.
    • Pot the fan leads now if you are running them: the Ø6 pass is in the strip between the heatsink seat and the shroud wall, with a dam on the inner face to hold the compound.

  4. Display into the shell, then the cover on

    • Bond the panel into the front shell’s seat. The rails and side pads locate it; leave the 0.5 mm bond line — do not squeeze it to nothing.
    • Lay the 3 mm cord into the groove in the shell’s brim. Scarf the ends and glue the joint away from the bottom edge.
    • Cover on. 4 × M3 panel screws first, through the cover’s bearing posts into the display’s own standoffs, each with a bonded washer.
    • Then 22 × M3 brim screws at 45 mm pitch. These are outboard of the cord and go into blind pilots, so they are not a leak path — but pack each one with Tef-Gel: a blind hole full of seawater around a 316 thread is a crevice.
    • Do this before the shroud. Four of the brim screws end up underneath it.

  5. Fans and mesh into the shroud, shroud onto the cover

    • Lay the 316 mesh on the eight fan bosses inside the shroud, then both 80 mm fans on top of it. The fan screws clamp the mesh — there is no separate retainer.
    • Check the fans blow inward, onto the fins. The louvres are the intake.
    • Shroud onto the cover and 4 × M3 × 40 from the louvred face — the only face you can still reach — down the corner bosses into the cover’s pilot bosses. They stop blind; they never enter the box.

  6. Bail arms onto the trunnions

    • The unit does not drop between the arms. It is 334 mm wide and the arms’ inner faces are 320 apart. Each arm goes on sideways, from outboard, and the unit + arms assembly then goes onto the base.
    • Per side, outboard to inboard: knob or cap screw → arm eye → 316 serrated washer pair → trunnion land → the M5 nyloc captive in the 8 mm hex pocket in the web.
    • The serrated pair is what holds the tilt — teeth, not friction. Set it by hand: firm enough to stay, loose enough to move.
    • Fit the M5 heat-set inserts into the arms’ foot pads now, while the arms are still loose and you can get an iron square to them.

  7. Onto the base plate, then the dash

    • Lower the unit and arms onto the base plate. Bolt the feet from underneath — M5 × 16 up through the plate into the inserts in the pads. This is why the arms go on the plate before the plate goes on the dash.
    • Then the plate to the dash through its 6 slots, in two rows 68 mm apart so the rows take the peel moment.
    • Screw the whip onto the SMA on top of the +x bump.
    • Tilt range is 0° to 20°, face up only. The arms run inside the bezel width, so face-down swings the unit into them.
